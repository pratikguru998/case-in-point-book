# 🧮 Math drills

Case math isn't hard; it's **fast, calculator-free and full of zeros**. Practise until percentages and breakevens feel automatic. Answers within ~5% count: that's how you'd round in a real case.

<MathDrill />

## Formulas that show up again and again

| Concept | Formula | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Percent change | (new − old) ÷ old | Stock $15 → $18 = **+20%** |
| Market share | our sales ÷ market | $30M of $170M ≈ **18%** |
| Profit | revenue − costs = (price − unit cost) × volume | Harley: 330K × $10K = **$3.3B** |
| Breakeven units | fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit | $500K ÷ $1.50 ≈ **333K** |
| Payback | investment ÷ annual savings | $1M ÷ $200K = **5 years** |
| ROI | (gain − investment) ÷ investment | (105 − 100) ÷ 100 = **5%** |
| Compound growth | `PV × (1 + g)^n` | $11B at 5% for 5 yrs ≈ **$14B** |
| Expected value | Σ probability × outcome | 0.4 × $20K + 0.6 × $0 = **$8K** |
| Required growth | target ÷ current − 1 | 772 ÷ 660 − 1 ≈ **17%** |
| Margin | profit ÷ revenue | — |
| Markup | profit ÷ cost | $1 cost, $2 price = **100% markup** |

## Shortcuts

- **Rule of 72:** years to double ≈ 72 ÷ growth rate (5% → ~14 years).
- **Compounding in your head:** 5% a year for 5 years is a bit more than 25% → call it ~28%.
- **Percent of:** 18% of 45 = 45% of 18 (swap if it's easier).
- **Fractions:** 1/3 ≈ 33%, 1/6 ≈ 17%, 1/7 ≈ 14%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 ≈ 11%.
- **Zeros:** multiply the digits, then add up the zeros. Say the unit out loud (million or billion) before you say the number.
- **Sanity check:** does it make sense? If not, redo it *before* you speak.
