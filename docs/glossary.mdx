# 🔤 Glossary

Short definitions in plain language. The number in brackets is the chapter (or section) where the term is explained or used.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Airport test** | Would the interviewer enjoy being stuck with you for hours? A test of fit (2) |
| **Barriers to entry** | Things that make it hard to enter a market: capital, regulation, distribution access, switching costs, scale (4, 5) |
| **Barriers to exit** | Things that make it hard to leave a market (contracts, specialised assets) (4) |
| **BCG matrix** | Growth-share grid: stars, cash cows, question marks, pets (5) |
| **Benchmarking** | Comparing your costs or metrics with competitors' (4) |
| **Breakeven** | Volume at which profit is zero: fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit (6.8) |
| **Build / buy / JV** | The three ways to enter a market: organically, by acquisition, or by joint venture (4) |
| **Cannibalisation** | A new product taking sales from your own existing products (4, 6.3) |
| **Case start** | Practising just the opening and structure of a case (6.2) |
| **Churn** | Share of customers lost in a period (6.2, 6.4) |
| **Cost-based pricing** | Price = costs + margin (4) |
| **Competitive response** | How rivals react to your move, or how you react to theirs (4) |
| **Due diligence** | Checking a target's health, markets, people and risks before buying (4) |
| **E(P=R−C)M** | The P&L structure: economy, market, then profit = revenue − costs (4) |
| **Economies of scale** | Lower unit cost at higher volume (6.3, 6.7) |
| **Expected value** | Probability-weighted average outcome (6.5) |
| **Factor question** | "What factors would you consider…?" Answer broadly (3) |
| **Final slide** | A summary table drawn at the start and filled in as you go (3) |
| **Five Forces** | Porter: entrants, rivalry, substitutes, buyers, suppliers (5) |
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue − cost of goods) ÷ revenue (5, 6.9) |
| **Hypothesis** | Your assumed answer, used to focus the analysis (4) |
| **Idea box** | A space on page 1 of your notes for ideas to use later (3) |
| **Interviewee-led case** | Broad "what should we do?" case; you drive the structure (4) |
| **Interviewer-led case** | "Find these four things" case; the list is the structure (4) |
| **JIT (just in time)** | Holding minimal inventory and receiving supplies as needed (5, 6.5) |
| **Market sizing** | Estimating a market from logical assumptions: population, household or preposterous (3) |
| **Operating margin** | Profit after cost of goods and SG&A ÷ revenue (5, 6.9) |
| **Partition pricing** | Charging separately for delivery, installation and so on instead of one bundled price (4) |
| **Payback period** | Investment ÷ annual savings or profit (6.6, 6.9) |
| **Payout ratio** | Dividends ÷ net profit (6.9) |
| **PEI** | Personal Experience Interview: questions about your past behaviour (2) |
| **Price-based costing** | Start from what customers will pay, then design costs to fit (4) |
| **Private label** | A store's own brand, often made by an established manufacturer (6.8) |
| **ROI** | (Gain − investment) ÷ investment (6.7) |
| **Seasonal balance** | Adding products that sell in other seasons to smooth revenue (4) |
| **SG&A** | Selling, general and administrative expenses (6.9) |
| **Stress interview** | Interviewers deliberately rude, silent or contradictory to test composure (2) |
| **Switching costs** | What a customer loses by changing supplier; raising them protects share (5, 6.3) |
| **Value chain** | Inbound → operations → delivery → marketing & sales → service (5) |
| **Vertical integration** | Owning more steps of your supply chain (6.2) |
| **What counts as success?** | The client's own measure of success, used to reality-test the objective (4) |
